# Replace RAG similarity with task-shaped SEO models

The garbage suggestions in these pipelines come mostly from a framing error, not a weak model. **Only one of the six problems is query-to-passage retrieval.** Topical overlap and intent similarity are *symmetric* similarity tasks. Link-worthiness is *directed link prediction*. Social and lead matching are *instruction-conditioned relevance*, which means the criteria change on every call and often contain negations. Page type is *supervised multi-class classification*. Each shape has a better open-source starting point than a RAG embedder. The recommended starting points are Qwen3-Embedding in its symmetric mode, hashing for near-duplicates, anchor dictionaries and co-citation for link candidates, the instruction-trained Qwen3-Reranker for criteria matching, and WebOrganizer's page-format classifier as a teacher for page type. None of them is SEO-specific, because **no public SEO-trained embedder, reranker, intent-similarity model or page-role dataset exists**. The closing move is therefore to train small models on labels the product already produces. Those labels are DataForSEO SERP overlap for intent, crawled in-body links for link-worthiness, GSC query overlap for cannibalization, and above all the logged verdicts of the final LLM review for every pipeline. Jev can be replaced for the fixed questions by a distilled 150–400M encoder that runs on a CPU or a T4. For open-ended yes/no screens, a fine-tuned Qwen3-Reranker can replace it, with DiffusionGemma-as-Jev as the self-hosted zero-shot teacher. Laya works as a fine-tuning base, not as a drop-in model. Training compute is trivial (under an hour per model on one consumer GPU). The real cost is building a gold set per task, and that is where the experiment plan below starts.

## Six problems, four machine-learning shapes

A RAG embedder is trained so that a passage lands near the query it *answers*. That objective puts a broad hub page close to every specific page under it. It also puts a definition page far from a product page that should link to it. It cannot represent "not an agency" at all. The first step is to name what each problem actually is, because the name decides the model class, the loss, the label source and the metric.

| # | Problem | Formal framing | Symmetric? | Natural label source | Metric that matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Content overlap / cannibalization / near-duplicate | Semantic textual similarity plus pair classification (duplicate yes/no); clustering for pillar grouping | Yes, sim(A,B) = sim(B,A) | MinHash for near-dups; shared GSC queries between URLs; LLM verdicts | Precision at fixed recall on pairs; cluster purity |
| 2 | Search-intent similarity (keyword↔keyword, page↔page) | Symmetric short-text metric learning with *behavioral* ground truth (SERP overlap); intent labels are multi-class classification | Yes for kw↔kw and page↔page; page↔query is asymmetric | DataForSEO top-10 URL overlap; ORCAS-I labels (research only) | AUC against SERP-overlap labels |
| 3 | Should page A link to page B (reference, next read, prerequisite, product) | Directed link prediction on a document graph, with anchor-level entity linking as a sub-task; positive-unlabeled learning | No, the ordered pair (A→B) matters | Existing in-body internal links (noisy positives); Common Crawl anchors; LLM verdicts | Candidate-generator Recall@K on held-out links; precision at threshold |
| 4 | Social post relevant to a domain description; content vs. a "what we want" prompt | Instruction-conditioned binary relevance (the criteria are an input, not a fixed class) | No, (criteria, item) | User keep/dismiss; LLM review verdicts | Recall at a target threshold; pass-through rate |
| 5 | Lead profile matches a prose ICP | The same instruction-conditioned relevance, *plus* structured constraint satisfaction on firmographics | No | Keep/dismiss logs; LLM verdicts; PDL company data for synthetic pairs | Recall at threshold after hard filters |
| 6 | Page type (money, product, docs, informational, …) | Fixed-label multi-class classification (7 labels in the repo today) | n/a | LLM/Jev teacher labels; WebOrganizer formats; schema.org types | Macro-F1; calibrated confidence |

Two findings shape everything after this table. First, the benchmark categories that measure problems 1 and 2 are MTEB's **STS, Clustering and Pair Classification**, not Retrieval. On those categories model rankings are flatter than on retrieval, and they sometimes invert ([Qwen3-Embedding card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)). Second, an August 2026 study of 26 embedders against 10 LLMs over 37 tasks found that **LLMs and embedders "match on clustering, STS, and pair classification"**. The embedders win on classification, and an LLM pass cost up to **1,431x more** (USD 154 vs USD 0.11 per benchmark pass) ([arXiv 2608.12875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12875)). That validates the user's cascade: similarity and classification belong in cheap models, and the LLM earns its cost only on the final judgement. What has to change is the *kind* of cheap model in each slot.

## Symmetric problems want symmetric prompts and behavioral labels

### Topical overlap: prompting mode matters more than a bigger model

On the symmetric MTEB categories the gap between a 0.6B and an 8B model is small. **Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B scores STS 86.57, Clustering 54.05 and PairClass 84.37. The 8B scores 88.58, 58.57 and 87.52.** On pair classification, the older **gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct (87.52) and multilingual-e5-large-instruct (86.24) beat Qwen3-0.6B**, even though they trail it by 8–11 points on retrieval ([Qwen3-Embedding card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)). The practical conclusion is that input choice decides the result more than model size. Input choice here means what text is embedded (boilerplate-stripped body, or title + H1 + headings) and which task prompt is used. The most likely single cause of today's garbage candidates is embedding one page as `query` and the other as `passage`. That asymmetric setup rewards "B answers A", which is exactly how a hub page ends up near everything. Both jina-embeddings-v3 and EmbeddingGemma make the split explicit. Jina v3 has a `text-matching` adapter for STS and symmetric retrieval, separate from `retrieval.query`/`retrieval.passage` ([jina v3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10173)). EmbeddingGemma ships `task: sentence similarity` and `task: clustering` prompts ([EmbeddingGemma card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-300m)).

The permissively licensed shortlist is short. **Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B** (Apache-2.0, 32k context, instructions, Matryoshka dimensions 32–1024) is the default because its context holds whole pages. **Qwen3-Embedding-4B** adds about 3.5 points on STS and clustering when a GPU is available. **EmbeddingGemma-300M** suits CPU and edge use, but its Gemma license is custom and its context stops at 2,048 tokens ([EmbeddingGemma card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-300m)). Exclude **jina-embeddings-v4 (CC-BY-NC-4.0)** and NV-Embed-v2 for a commercial product ([BentoML guide](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/a-guide-to-open-source-embedding-models)). jina v3's license was not confirmed in this research, and it is believed to be non-commercial as well.

Near-duplicates are a separate problem, and hashing handles them better. Screaming Frog, the de facto crawler reference, uses **MinHash on boilerplate-stripped text at a 90% default** for near-duplicates. It runs a separate embedding-based "Semantically Similar" filter at **cosine 0.95** for paraphrased cannibalization ([SF Near Duplicates](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/issues/content/near-duplicates/); [SF Semantically Similar](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/issues/content/semantically-similar/)). The layered design follows directly. Exact hashes come first, then MinHash LSH at 0.9 or above, which labels near-duplicates with no model. Embeddings then run only on the pairs left over. Hub-page false positives go through a **mutual-kNN filter** (keep A–B only if each is in the other's top-k). The pairs that survive then go to the decision stage. Absolute cosine thresholds do not transfer between models, because e5, bge and Qwen3 have very different cosine distributions. Calibrate a threshold per model on labelled pairs, or use rank-based candidate generation instead of a global cutoff.

Content similarity cannot tell "best X" from "X price". Two pages on the same topic can serve different intents, and only behavioral evidence separates them. For cannibalization that evidence is **query overlap between two URLs in GSC**, the site-level equivalent of SERP overlap. GSC stays in the app, so the app has to send the shared-query counts along with the candidates.

### Intent similarity: the product already buys the ground truth

Practitioners treat SERP overlap as the ground truth for intent. Keywords that share **3 or more of the same top-10 URLs** belong together, and keywords that share 0–1 do not ([Nightwatch](https://nightwatch.io/blog/keyword-clustering/); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/keyword-clustering)). Graded bands run from 7–10 shared URLs (same page) down to 0–1 (separate) ([seo-cluster](https://claude-seo.md/skills/seo-cluster)). Vendors say plainly that embeddings "measure linguistic relatedness rather than confirmed search-engine behavior" ([KeyClusters](https://www.keyclusters.com/blog/semantic-keyword-clustering)). The published version of learning intent from behavior is Bing's **GEN Encoder**, which trained query embeddings on **about 200M co-clicked query groups** and cut unseen queries by about 50% via ANN ([arXiv 1907.10710](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10710)). Its weights were never released, and **no open "search-intent similarity" embedder exists on Hugging Face**.

Prompting will not close the gap. Qwen3 gains 1–5% from instructions in general. Its FollowIR-style instruction-sensitivity score is only **5.09 for the 0.6B**, and multilingual-e5-large-instruct scores **−0.40** ([Qwen3-Embedding card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)). "Same intent, not same topic" is too subtle for an instruction to express. **Fine-tuning is the lever.** Because the api already fetches SERPs through the seo-data facade, the product can build a small GEN Encoder. Mine keyword pairs whose top-10 URLs overlap as positives. Take pairs that embed close but share no SERP URLs as hard negatives. Fine-tune Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B with MultipleNegativesRanking or CoSENT on the graded overlap. At inference a new keyword then needs no SERP call. For intent *labels* (navigational, transactional, informational), **ORCAS-I** provides 2M weakly labelled queries and a 1,000-query gold set ([arXiv 2205.00926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00926)). It is research-only, because it inherits the MS MARCO/ORCAS terms ([MS MARCO Terms](https://microsoft.github.io/msmarco/Notice.html)), and it has no "commercial investigation" class. In an ORCAS-I comparison, LLM intent labelers beat weak supervision on recall but **"struggle with precision"** ([arXiv 2504.21398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21398)). That is one more reason to keep the LLM as reviewer rather than first-pass labeller.

## Link-worthiness is directed link prediction, and cosine is the wrong generator

"Should A link to B" has three components that the literature treats separately. The first is anchor-level entity linking (this phrase in A should point to B). The second is document-level relatedness learned from links or citations. The third is graph link prediction. **No open model is trained for "should A link to B" on general websites.** The closest building blocks learn from link or citation supervision. **SPECTER2** (Apache-2.0) trained on over 6M citation triplets and has a proximity adapter for link prediction ([HF specter2_base](https://huggingface.co/allenai/specter2_base)). **SciNCL** samples positives and hard negatives from neighbourhoods in a citation-graph embedding and gains 1.8 points on SciDocs ([arXiv 2202.06671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06671)). **LinkBERT** pretrains with a document-relation head that separates contiguous, random and *linked* segments ([arXiv 2203.15827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15827)). **PHP** grades pairs as no link, one-way link, reciprocal link or highly relevant reciprocal link, and those grades map directly onto link-worthiness labels ([arXiv 2209.06583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06583)). All of these are trained on science or Wikipedia text and will transfer poorly to commercial pages. **Their recipes transfer; their weights do not.** Most are also symmetric bi-encoders, so real directionality has to come from a cross-encoder over the ordered pair (source passage, target page), or from an instruction-conditioned query embedding.

The one production system with published numbers is not neural at all. Wikimedia's Add-a-link builds an **anchor dictionary** from existing links and scores (mention, target) pairs with a **7-feature XGBoost** model. The features are anchor frequency, ambiguity, Levenshtein distance to the target title, and embedding distance. It reaches **81.3% precision at 45.0% recall on English Wikipedia**, with a deployment bar of 75% precision ([Wikimedia research](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Research:Link_recommendation_model_for_add-a-link_structured_task)). Hard-coded target filters (no disambiguation pages, lists or years) remove whole classes of bad targets. In an A/B test the tool lifted mobile constructive activation by 33.7% and cut reverts by 19.6% ([Wikimedia A/B report](https://analytics.wikimedia.org/published/reports/growth/add_a_link_enwiki_ab_test_report.html)). InLinks works the same way commercially: users assign entities to target pages, and links come from pages that mention those entities ([InLinks help](https://inlinks.com/help/how-to-automate-your-internal-linking/)). The anchor-dictionary approach catches the cases cosine misses, such as a "definition page for a term used here" or a "product page mentioned in this how-to". On a site, the dictionary comes from existing internal anchors, each page's title and H1, and its GSC queries. The SEO equivalents of Wikimedia's filters are tag archives, pagination, legal and utility pages, and near-duplicates.

Graph features should prioritise, not score. On Wikipedia subsets, DeepWalk (graph) and LSA (text) predicted editor-added links about equally well. **Text-plus-graph hybrids did not improve on either** ([arXiv 2105.11734](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.11734v1)), and Wikimedia dropped its one behavioral graph feature as negligible. On a small templated site graph, node2vec mostly learns the navigation template. Graph signals such as target inlink count (to boost orphans), click depth, existing-link dedup and reciprocal links belong in ranking and constraints. The exception is **co-citation**, a strong *candidate generator* for related-but-dissimilar pages. If pages X and Y are both linked from the same sources, Y is a "see also" candidate for pages that link to X ([Schwarzer et al., JCDL 2016](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2910896.2910908)). A cheap addition on the embedding side, not yet benchmarked, is one Qwen3 query embedding per relation type against a single page index. The instructions would read like "retrieve the pages a reader needs as background" and "retrieve the product page this how-to should link to". Recall paths multiply with no extra indexing.

Web hyperlinks can serve as free supervision if they are filtered hard. Anchor-DR trained on web anchor→document pairs and gained **+5.3% NDCG@10 on MS MARCO** over the prior unsupervised state of the art. It removed nav, header and footer anchors, functional anchors ("homepage") and non-query-like anchors, and it **dropped same-site anchors** ([arXiv 2305.05834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05834)), which are exactly the internal links this product cares about. Internal-link training data therefore needs its own filters. Keep only main-content links, drop links present on more than 30–50% of a site's pages, drop "read more" style anchors, and use the surrounding sentence as the source passage. Treat unlinked pairs as *unlabeled*, not negative, because most link-worthy pairs are simply never linked.

## Criteria matching needs a model that reads criteria and item together

Problems 4 and 5 differ in kind from the rest. The target is a paragraph of criteria that changes per user and often says "not agencies" or "must mention pricing pain". The negation evidence is decisive. On NevIR, where random is 25% pairwise accuracy, **bi-encoders score 6.5–19.8% and Qwen-based embedders 11.8–19.0%, below random**. BGE rerankers reach 32–44%, jina-reranker-v2 65.2%, GPT-4o 70.1% and o3-mini 77.3% ([arXiv 2502.13506](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506v1)). Bi-encoder document vectors are "nearly identical despite negation words" ([NevIR](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.139/)). **Embeddings can only provide recall here: loose threshold, large k, never used as the filter.**

The right model for the middle slot is an instruction-trained reranker that outputs P(yes). **Qwen3-Reranker** (Apache-2.0, 32k context) scores a yes/no logit difference that a sigmoid turns into a probability. Its FollowIR instruction-sensitivity scores are **5.41 (0.6B), 14.84 (4B) and 8.05 (8B)**, against **−0.01 for bge-reranker-v2-m3**, meaning the classic rerankers ignore instruction changes entirely ([Qwen3-Reranker card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B)). Put the domain description or ICP in the instruction and the post or profile in the document, then read P(yes). Reasoning rerankers help on hard negations and on the borderline band. **Rank1-7B scores 65.1 on NevIR and Rank1-32B scores 70.1, matching GPT-4o**, and Rank1-0.5B is comparable to RankLLaMA-13B ([Rank1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18418)). But Rank1 writes up to 1,000 tokens per judgement and can be over-strict. It rejected every passage on one TREC-COVID query, which is dangerous in a recall stage. Bi-encoders *can* learn instruction-following: Promptriever gained **+14.3 p-MRR** by training on "instruction negatives", meaning the same query with an instruction that makes a relevant passage irrelevant ([arXiv 2409.11136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11136)). Its training data is MS MARCO-derived and cannot ship commercially, but the recipe can be reproduced on the product's own data.

A cheaper alternative is to decompose the criteria. Once per criteria, an LLM splits the ICP or topic prompt into 3–8 atomic hypotheses, such as "the author runs a marketing agency" and "the author complains about pricing". An NLI model such as **deberta-v3-large-zeroshot-v2.0** (435M, 512 tokens) scores each hypothesis per item ([model card](https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/deberta-v3-large-zeroshot-v2.0)). Alternatively, **GLiClass** scores all hypotheses in one forward pass ([GLiClass](https://github.com/knowledgator/gliclass)). A boolean rule makes the decision: AND the musts, and reject if any exclusion scores "yes". This pattern is inferred, not benchmarked, but it turns every negation into a *positive* hypothesis, the form every model handles best. For leads, **firmographic constraints (headcount, industry, geography) are hard filters on enriched fields and should never reach a text model.** Only the soft parts ("founders struggling with …") need semantic matching. No public benchmark exists for social-listening relevance or prose-ICP matching. Commercial tools such as Octolens use the same LLM-scoring-plus-feedback loop ([Octolens](https://octolens.com/keyword-monitoring)), and 2026 lead-scoring research targets CRM conversion, not ICP matching ([arXiv 2606.04387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04387)). **This product's own keep/dismiss logs are the dataset.**

## Page type is a distillation problem with a ready-made teacher

The repo asks Jev one 7-way `page_type` choice over url + title + meta + about 8,000 characters of body, and discards answers below 0.7 confidence (`api/src/site_pages/llm_extraction/questions.py`). With a fixed label set and a few hundred labels, the evidence strongly favors a fine-tuned encoder. Fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-class models beat zero-shot GPT-4 on specialized tasks (stance **0.94 vs 0.51 F1**), and gains **saturate after about 200–500 labels** ([arXiv 2406.08660](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08660v1)). A 2026 cost study found fine-tuned encoders **1–10x faster and 100–400x cheaper** per million predictions than Claude or GPT-4o, at comparable or better macro-F1 ([arXiv 2602.06370](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06370v1)). At equal training, a 150M Ettin encoder beats a 400M decoder on MNLI ([Ettin](https://huggingface.co/blog/ettin)).

The closest ready-made asset is **WebOrganizer's FormatClassifier**, a 140M gte-base model with `{url}\n{text}` input. It was distilled from Llama-3.1-8B labels on 1M documents, then Llama-3.1-405B labels on 100K. Its **24 formats** include Product Page, Content Listing, Documentation, FAQ, Customer Support, About (Org.), Legal Notices, Tutorial and Knowledge Article ([HF FormatClassifier](https://huggingface.co/WebOrganizer/FormatClassifier); [arXiv 2502.10341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10341)). Its label annotations are public. It maps well onto the repo's taxonomy: Product Page→money, Content Listing→hub, Knowledge Article/Tutorial/Listicle/News→informational, FAQ/Documentation/Support→support, About/News (Org.)→company, Legal→legal. It has **no pricing, landing or utility (login, cart, search, pagination) class**, and its license and accuracy were not on the model card, so check both before commercial use. **No public dataset labels SEO page roles.** Weak labels are available from schema.org types (Web Data Commons publishes class-specific extractions ([WDC](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3543873.3587331))), URL path tokens, link density, and the internal PageRank the repo already computes. The utility classes are nearly deterministic from URL and DOM rules and should not need a model.

## A self-trained Jev: distil into encoders, keep diffusion Gemma as teacher

**Jev** is TypeSafe's closed, non-generative "System One" decision model. It has choice, noul (yes/no) and score primitives, a 32k context, and costs **$0.042 per million input tokens with free output**. No weights, paper, architecture or fine-tuning option have been published ([OpenRouter blog](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/what-is-jev/); [Jev docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/community/jev)). Third parties measured **236–276 ms per call** ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/convai-ships-laya-a-non-autoregressive-multilingual-system-1-decision-model-at)). Its "confidence" measures how concentrated the distribution is, not P(correct). The repo's fixed 0.7 cutoff is therefore not a calibrated recall control.

The **"diffusion Gemma decision model"** is a community pattern, not a Google product. It runs Google's Apache-2.0 **DiffusionGemma 26B-A4B** (MoE, 4B active, about 18 GB quantized) as a Jev-compatible server ([Google overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/diffusiongemma)). **OpenJev** masks only the answer slots in the diffusion canvas, runs one denoising pass and reads the label-token logits. Confidence is 1 − H(p)/ln K, and high-entropy cases are re-read with other seeds. It reports **about 31 ms per 3-question request and 57 req/s at 64 concurrency** on a workstation GPU, and 0.2–0.4 s on an M3 Ultra via MLX ([OpenJev](https://github.com/razorback16/openjev)). Accuracy evidence is thin. A local run reportedly scored **84.8% vs Jev's 86.6% on the public 231-item JevBench**, but that figure rests on a search snippet ([awesome-jev #15](https://github.com/heyjunpenn/awesome-jev/issues/15)), and no fine-tuning recipe for DiffusionGemma as a classifier is documented. **Laya** (Convai, Apache-2.0, released 2026-09-19) is a 421M ModernBERT-large decision model with a **512-token context**, plus a 322M multilingual variant with 1,024 tokens. It runs at **39.5 ms on a T4**. It is **near-random zero-shot (0.362 vs 0.318 random)**, reaches 0.766 after fine-tuning on a benchmark's own training split (Jev: 0.727), and **collapses past about 20 options** (Banking77 0.425 vs Jev 0.870). Its raw ECE of 0.466 falls to 0.081 only after temperature refitting ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/laya-ai); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/convai-ships-laya-a-non-autoregressive-multilingual-system-1-decision-model-at)). These are all vendor-reported numbers with no independent replication.

| Role | Best-fit model | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed questions (page_type, pillar/link screens with stable wording) | Fine-tuned ModernBERT-base/large or Ettin-150/400M, one head per question (or a Laya-style option-marker head), long-context variant for 2k-token pages | Encoders win at 200–500+ labels; CPU/T4 cost; WebOrganizer proves the distillation recipe at web scale | Needs recalibration; Laya-EN's 512 tokens truncates today's input |
| Open-ended yes/no screens (criteria vary per call) | Fine-tuned Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B (4B if quality matters), P(yes) output | Instruction-trained, Apache-2.0, continuous calibratable score, already shaped like "question + state → yes/no" | Slower per pair than an encoder (about 830 ms per 20 pairs unoptimized ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/rerankers))) |
| Zero-shot teacher and fallback for questions with no training data | DiffusionGemma via OpenJev (self-hosted), or Jev itself | Closest open match to Jev's generality; speaks the Jev wire protocol | 24 GB GPU; accuracy evidence is a snippet |
| Borderline band only | Rank1-0.5B/1.5B | Reasoning helps on negation; its rationale goes to the reviewer | Up to 1,000 output tokens; over-strictness |

The existing `JsonDataExtractorAdapter` abstraction already takes state plus typed questions and returns probabilities. OpenJev and a local encoder server can therefore sit in the facade's ordered adapter list as a primary or fallback with little change. The recommended design is **Jev as teacher today and a distilled encoder as the primary adapter tomorrow**, with Jev or OpenJev kept for new questions until they have enough labels.

## Training data the product already pays for

The cheapest credible labels are ones the product already generates. For **intent**, DataForSEO SERP overlap gives graded 0–10 shared-URL targets for CoSENT, or buckets (≥4 shared positive, ≤1 negative) for MNRL. For **cannibalization and topical overlap**, use shared GSC queries between URLs and same-section pairs, relabelled by an LLM on disagreement in the Gecko style ([DeepMind Gecko](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/85521/)). For **links**, use filtered in-body internal links from crawled sites, with Common Crawl WAT anchors for scale ([anchor-extraction code](https://github.com/rjonesx/common-crawl-anchor-text-extraction)). For **page type**, use Jev or WebOrganizer teacher labels plus schema.org types. For **leads**, the **People Data Labs free company dataset is CC BY 4.0** and usable commercially with attribution ([PDL](https://docs.peopledatalabs.com/docs/free-company-dataset)), paired with LLM-written ICPs. **Amazon ESCI** (Apache-2.0, 2.6M graded query–product judgments) is the one large commercial-friendly relevance set ([esci-data](https://github.com/amazon-science/esci-data)). **MS MARCO, ORCAS, ORCAS-I and Promptriever's data are research-only** and must not go into a shipped model. For every task, **each final-LLM-review verdict and each user keep/dismiss is a labelled triple**, and these logs are the most valuable asset once they are stored with the upstream scores. Reddit API terms and Common Crawl copyright need a legal check before those sources are used for training.

The volumes needed are modest. Databricks fine-tuned embedders on **6,000–14,220 synthetic pairs** and raised gte-large Recall@10 from **0.293 to 0.552** on FinanceBench. Gains shrank where the base model was already strong: e5-mistral gained nothing on DocsQA ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/improving-retrieval-and-rag-embedding-model-finetuning)). SetFit was competitive with full fine-tuning at **8 examples per class** ([arXiv 2209.11055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11055)). A **150M ModernBERT reranker** trained in-domain beat all 13 baselines, including a 1.5B model, and trained in **about 30 minutes on one RTX 3090** ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/train-reranker)). FinMTEB found MTEB scores **uncorrelated** with domain performance ([arXiv 2409.18511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18511)), so leaderboard rank predicts little for SEO tasks, and only an internal gold set can decide. Expect the biggest gains where general models have never seen the concept: SERP-defined intent, directed link-worthiness and SEO page roles. Expect small gains on generic topical overlap.

The training recipe is the same across tasks. First, sample candidate pairs from what the embed stage *actually* passes, and label them with the strong LLM as yes/no plus a 0–1 score, keeping the rationale for Amazon-style rationale distillation ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/publications/rationale-guided-distillation-for-e-commerce-relevance-classification-bridging-large-language-models-and-lightweight-cross-encoders)). Mix in the weak behavioral labels. Mine hard negatives with a margin: skip the top 10, cap similarity at 0.8 and keep a 0.1 margin, because the top of the list is full of unlabeled positives. Mix in random negatives, because hard negatives alone hurt easy cases ([sbert.net](https://sbert.net/docs/cross_encoder/training_overview.html)). Train bi-encoders with CachedMNRL or GISTEmbed plus Matryoshka, and train cross-encoders with BCE on soft teacher probabilities using `pos_weight` ([sbert.net losses](https://sbert.net/docs/sentence_transformer/training_overview.html)). For criteria tasks, add Promptriever-style instruction negatives: the same post, labelled differently under two different criteria. Choose thresholds on a held-out gold set at a **target recall of 0.95–0.98**, never at 0.5 or a fixed confidence. Then close the loop. Log every student score beside the LLM verdict, retrain on disagreements, and **send a random slice of student rejects to the LLM**, because otherwise the student's false negatives are never seen. Rented H100s cost about **$2–3 per hour** ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/h100-rental-prices-cloud-comparison)), so a training run costs single-digit dollars, and LLM labeling dominates the budget.

## A prioritized experiment plan

Every experiment below uses the same evaluation. Build a **gold set of about 1,000 pairs per task**: LLM-labelled, human-checked on disagreements and on a random slice. Report per-stage recall, pass-through rate, precision at fixed recall, and **LLM-review calls saved at equal end-to-end recall**. End-to-end recall is the product of the stage recalls. The order puts cheap, no-training fixes first and the most expensive custom models last.

| Priority | Experiment | What to run | Data | Success criterion | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Instrument the flywheel | Persist every candidate's embed score, Jev P(yes)/confidence, LLM verdict and user keep/dismiss per pipeline; build the six gold sets | Existing traffic | Gold sets exist; per-stage recall and pass-through are measurable | Days, no GPU |
| P1 | Fix symmetric prompting and layering (problems 1, 2) | Re-embed with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B in similarity/clustering mode on both sides; add MinHash LSH ≥0.9 for near-dups; add a mutual-kNN filter; calibrate a per-model threshold | Gold pairs + GSC shared-query counts | Higher precision at equal recall than the current query/passage setup; fewer hub-page candidates | Hours |
| P2 | Measure Jev's real recall and recalibrate | Plot Jev P(yes) for gold positives vs negatives per screen; replace the 0.7 cutoff with a recall-targeted threshold | Gold sets | Documented recall at the current and new threshold | Hours |
| P3 | Qwen3-Reranker as the criteria screen (problems 4, 5) | Zero-shot Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B and 4B with criteria as instruction vs Jev on the same pairs; add the NLI-decomposition variant and hard firmographic filters for leads | Social and lead gold sets | Equal or higher recall at ≥ Jev precision; throughput of thousands of pairs/min on one GPU | 1–2 days |
| P4 | Link candidate generators (problem 3) | Hold out 20% of in-body links per source page; measure Recall@K for cosine, anchor dictionary, co-citation and relation-instruction queries, alone and as a union | Crawled sites' filtered in-body links | The union beats cosine alone on held-out recall, stratified by relation type | 2–3 days |

The plan is ordered so that each result decides the next step. P1 and P2 show how much of today's garbage comes from framing and miscalibration rather than from model capability. P3 and P4 show whether off-the-shelf instruction rerankers and non-cosine generators close the gap without training. Only then do P5–P7 spend labels on custom models, and P0 has been producing those labels all along. If P1–P4 already reach the target recall at an acceptable pass-through, P8 and P9 can wait.

## Conclusion

The shift is from "which embedder is best" to "which learning problem is this". Once each pipeline is named correctly, the RAG model is demoted to a recall generator in most of them. For the criteria and link pipelines it is not even the right generator: anchor dictionaries, co-citation and decomposed hypotheses find candidates that cosine structurally cannot. The deeper finding is that **the hyper-specific SEO models the user wants do not exist to download, and the data to build them already flows through the product**. SERP overlap, GSC query sharing, crawled links and LLM review verdicts are richer SEO supervision than any public dataset, and the commercially licensed public sets are few and weakly matched.

Two uncertainties deserve respect. Almost none of the SEO-specific precision and recall claims in the market are measured, so the product's own gold sets will be the first real numbers. The open Jev alternatives also rest on vendor or snippet evidence. The durable advantage is therefore not any single model but the flywheel. It logs every stage's score next to the final verdict, retrains the cheap stages on the disagreements, and audits what they reject. A competitor can download the same Qwen3 weights. It cannot download the accumulated verdicts.
